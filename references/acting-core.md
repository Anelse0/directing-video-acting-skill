# Complex-State Parsing

Use this reference only when the user's inner state is layered, contradictory, ambiguous, invented, or difficult to make playable. The main Skill owns the general compiler.

## Parse conflict as action

Do not map a state name to a standard face or body pattern. Facial and bodily cues are many-to-many and context-dependent. Instead, write a free-form internal plan:

```text
SCENE FACTS: immutable user facts
PATH: event-driven or sustained
OBJECTIVE OR TASK: what the character tries to obtain, protect, test, finish, or avoid
TRIGGER: only if something changes during the shot
COUNTERFORCE: an actual competing duty, belief, relationship need, or physical limit
DISPLAY CHOICE: only if revealing, containing, redirecting, or ignoring matters
PLAYABLE ACTION: behavior toward a person, object, task, or space
CUT STATE: the held condition, threshold, or action at the edit
```

Keep every slot as free text. Omit slots the scene does not need.

For an unfamiliar abstract state, ask internally:

1. What does this person try to make happen next?
2. What makes that action difficult or changes its meaning?
3. Which visible choice or action change can carry both forces without an anatomy inventory?

This handles a user-created state without naming or classifying it.

## Turn contradiction into a coherent playable strategy

Several emotions do not require several simultaneous expressions. Translate them into competing objectives, interpretations, or public/private actions.

- Choose a coherent public action already supported by the scene: continue the task, answer courteously, verify evidence, maintain distance, finish the ritual, or leave.
- Add a counterforce only when it exists in the scene.
- Start with the behavior that exposes the conflict: an action completes differently, stops, changes owner, or continues despite new meaning. Add detail only when it carries a distinct required beat.
- When several tactics could serve the same objective, choose the one that best carries the contradiction. Do not stack alternative tests, gestures, or invented partner compliance merely because runtime is unspecified.

Containment is playable only when the prompt defines what the character preserves: professional speech, another person's comfort, a routine, a boundary, or a practical task. `Hides emotion` alone supplies no action.

Avoid using `starts to ... then stops` as a universal sign of conflict. Interruption must affect an action the character plausibly intended to complete.

## Choose the carrier

Select a carrier that can do story work in the implied frame:

- gaze to a named person, object, task, or exit;
- completion, interruption, or transfer of a relevant object action;
- distance, orientation, weight, or direction of travel;
- speech beginning, changing, ending, or being withheld;
- stillness that contrasts with a previously active task;
- breath only when visually or audibly perceptible.

Bound it. `Looks around nervously`, `gestures emotionally`, `keeps trembling`, and `shows micro-expressions` are not bounded actions. Prefer constructions such as:

```text
She finishes fastening the last button, but leaves her hand there instead of reaching for the coat.
His prepared reply ends after the first word; he turns the document so the signature faces her.
The body stays oriented toward the exit while the gaze returns once to the child.
```

Do not assign truth, guilt, or a universal emotional meaning to an isolated cue. The objective and scene establish its meaning.

## Keep temporal scope honest

- A qualifier such as `at first`, `while speaking`, or `at the cut` governs only that interval.
- Do not invent a reaction transition in a sustained task.
- Do not add a processing pause to a reflex, interruption, or already-understood situation.
- Do not jump straight to a peak when comprehension or attempted control is the scene.
- Do not add an aftermath when the requested edit lands on the threshold.

If several required turns cannot remain legible, merge compatible behavior or allocate separate shots. Preserve the user's turns rather than selecting a favorite one silently.

## Example of derivation, not a template

Intent: a chef accepts an award while realizing that a rival deliberately withdrew.

Playable result:

```text
She takes the trophy with the practiced smile already expected of the winner. During the applause,
she notices the rival's untouched entry beside the judges' table. The smile remains for the cameras,
but her free hand lowers the trophy from chest height and no longer presents it outward. When the host
offers the microphone, she keeps her attention on the untouched plate for one moment, then accepts the
microphone without beginning the victory speech. Cut with the applause continuing and the trophy held
low at her side.
```

The public task continues while a concrete action changes its meaning. No emotion label or body-part inventory is required.
