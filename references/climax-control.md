# Climax and Threshold Control

## Define the climax

Treat a climax as a threshold after which the character cannot continue the same strategy, belief, relationship, or choice. Do not equate climax with maximum facial intensity, crying, shouting, collapse, or loss of control.

A threshold may be:

- confirmation that changes what the character knows;
- failure of a task, sentence, social mask, or recovery attempt;
- a confession, refusal, boundary, departure, contact, or request;
- a decision to continue containing the emotion;
- an external action that makes the relationship irreversible.

Choose the threshold from the scene's stakes. Do not classify the user into a preset climax type.

## Select the threshold

When the shot approaches a threshold rather than beginning inside it, answer only the applicable questions:

1. What strategy is working before the peak?
2. What new confirmation or pressure makes that strategy impossible or costly?
3. What action, failure, line, silence, or choice crosses the threshold?
4. What remains in the body, task, space, or relationship at the cut?

If the user deliberately starts inside the peak, begin there. Do not add an unwanted calm prelude. If the shot ends at the threshold, do not invent an aftermath.

## Build only the required path

Use this as a menu, not a mandatory sequence:

```text
working strategy or mask
-> confirmation or added pressure
-> optional containment or recovery attempt
-> optional precise leak, interruption, or contradiction
-> threshold action / failure / decision
-> optional short release
-> explicit cut state
```

Do not multiply recovery attempts, swallows, gaze breaks, or escalating breaths merely to manufacture progression. Add repetition only when it is the dramatic event and the duration can show it.

For restraint, define what the character preserves—finishing a sentence, protecting another person, remaining professional, completing a task—not merely what the character suppresses.

For confrontation, define what each person wants from the exchange. Intensity can come from a sudden reduction in movement or volume if it changes the partner's behavior.

For a panic-like or dysregulated episode, select only the functional disruption and coping, protective, or escape behavior that the scene requires. Do not stack every possible symptom or claim a medical diagnosis.

## Control peak behavior

Choose the smallest set of carriers that makes the threshold legible instead of making every channel peak together. A carrier may be:

- speech cannot continue;
- a carefully maintained task stops;
- distance changes irreversibly;
- an object is released, returned, or placed between characters;
- voice or body control fails once;
- the character finally says the shortest true line;
- the character refuses the expected release and acts instead.

Add support only when it is visible, causally linked, and the duration and framing can carry it. Preserve distinct user-required beats; merge or allocate shots rather than silently deleting them. Let peak duration follow the requested scene, and do not prolong it merely to display more symptoms.

Prefer:

```text
The second attempt at the sentence ends on the first word. She sets the keys down and steps away.
```

Over:

```text
She breaks down intensely, sobbing uncontrollably with dramatic trembling and desperate gestures.
```

Do not use `intensely`, `extremely`, `overwhelmingly`, and `uncontrollably` as a substitute for a threshold.

## Design the cut state

The end need only be explicit; it does not need to be calm or exhausted. Choose what the scene actually leaves behind:

- recovery remains incomplete;
- physical agitation continues but becomes purposeful;
- the character becomes numb or low-energy;
- the character resumes the task with altered behavior;
- a partner now owns the silence;
- the character continues moving toward an irreversible action;
- the edit cuts exactly on the line, look, contact, or departure.

Avoid returning to the opening smile or posture unless deliberate recovery is the story.

## Diagnose false climaxes

- **Peak begins on frame one unintentionally:** restore a readable start state or confirmation beat.
- **The scene grows louder but does not change:** replace intensity adjectives with a decision, failed function, or altered relationship.
- **Every channel peaks together:** remove redundant carriers and retain only those that make the threshold legible.
- **Crying becomes theatrical:** separate tears, breath, speech, face, and posture; retain only components that perform distinct scene work.
- **The peak lasts forever:** define the exact release or cut state.
- **The character always ends exhausted:** choose continued action, residual agitation, numbness, refusal, or a threshold cut when appropriate.
- **The climax feels delayed by formula:** remove processing or suppression when the character already understands and is ready to act.
- **Both characters climax simultaneously:** make one person's threshold the other person's next trigger, or split reaction shots.
